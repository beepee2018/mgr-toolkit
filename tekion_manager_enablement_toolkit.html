A Framework to Keep in Mind — ADKAR

The workshop was designed around the ADKAR change model. The three sections of this toolkit follow the same logic. Keeping ADKAR in mind helps you understand where your team is — and what they need from you next.

Section 1 — Workshop Outcomes

What the leadership team aligned on during the April 28–29 workshop. Share this with your managers to connect what they heard on April 17 to what was produced in the workshop and what is coming in May.

What we aligned on

The Identity Shift

The move from Customer Success to Customer Value Services is not a rename. It is a shift in what we are accountable for. CVMs own outcomes, not relationships. CVAs own platform behavior, not support tickets. That behavioral shift is the foundation of everything built this week.

What we validated

Dealer Maturity Model v1

A four-tier framework the leadership team owns and has stress-tested against real accounts. Tiers are Operating, Accelerating, Optimizing, and Leading. The maturity signal is module adoption depth — from less than 50% of purchased modules through full platform adoption and top-quartile performance.

What we resolved

Role Boundaries

CVM owns the dealer relationship and commercial outcome. CVA owns platform behavior and adoption data. VVA owns scaled engagement and maturity progression for volume accounts. The CVP is CVM-authored with required CVA input. Dealer-facing communication routes through the CVM.

What we built

The Tekion Value OS

Four connected tools: the Dealer Maturity Model, Customer Value Plan, Touchpoint Framework, and Customer Value Review. All four are designed to work together as a system. The tools exist as artifacts now. Phase 2 enablement teaches the team to use them.

Role Accountability Statements

Agreed in the workshop. Use these when your team asks what each role means in practice.

CVM Customer Value Manager

The CVM owns the dealer relationship, the Customer Value Plan, and the commercial outcome. They are accountable for renewal, growth, and risk — and for ensuring the CVA has the account context to contribute effectively. At Tier 1 and 2 accounts, the CVM is focused on adoption fundamentals and building momentum. At Tier 3 and 4, the CVM is having a strategic business conversation about the dealer's full platform value and future performance.

CVA Customer Value Architect

The CVA owns the dealer's platform behavior — adoption depth, usage patterns, and expansion readiness. They translate what the data shows into the account plan and bring the technical credibility that makes the CVR a real business conversation. The CVA contributes Sections 2 and 3 of the Customer Value Plan as a required input. A CVP without CVA data is a draft.

VVA Virtual Value Assistance

VVA delivers scaled value engagement for volume accounts — focused on self-sufficiency milestones and maturity progression. When a dealer reaches the threshold that warrants named CVM ownership, VVA owns the handoff. The escalation trigger is a maturity event, not a headcount decision. The full VVA operating model is a Phase 2 design item.

Section 2 — Team Conversation Guide

For use after the May 8 town hall and in direct team conversations. Mark leads the town hall. Your job is what comes after -- the smaller, more personal conversations where your team members process what they heard and need honest answers from their manager.

They need

Clarity without overpromising

Say what you know. Acknowledge what you don't. Follow up on what you don't have answers to. The April 17 meeting named this explicitly -- authenticity means not having all the answers but following up consistently.

They need

Calm in the room

Your emotional register sets theirs. Leaders who appear anxious about the change create anxious teams. Mark's phrase was right: settle the crowd. Even-keel and steady is the most important thing you can model right now.

They need

A picture of what's coming

The town hall announces the change. Your team conversations make it real. Use the timeline below to show them the path: what happens in May, when they get trained, when things go live.

They need

Permission to not have it all figured out

The tools are being built. The enablement is coming. Your team doesn't need to be operating the new model perfectly on May 2nd. Help them understand the sequence and that support is built into the plan.

Questions Your Team Will Ask — And How to Answer Them

These are the real questions. Answer them directly. If you don't know, say so and follow up.

All roles Is my job at risk? ►
No. This is a transformation of how we operate and what we're accountable for — not a reduction. The org is adding a new enablement layer and creating cleaner role definitions. Your job title and functional accountability are changing. Your position in the organization is not.
If pushed further
Be direct: the goal of this transformation is to grow the business, which means the team needs to succeed. A shrinking team is not the plan. What is changing is what success looks like.
CVM What does my job actually look like differently starting May 12th? ►
The title change is immediate. The behavioral shift is phased. The week of May 18 you will go through dedicated CVM enablement sessions that teach you the new tools and the new operating model. You will not be expected to run a full Customer Value Review on day one. What you are expected to do right away: start using the CVM language, begin building familiarity with your named accounts, and show up to the enablement sessions ready to engage.
CVA How is a CVA different from what I do today as a CSE? ►
The core shift is from reactive technical support to proactive platform expertise. A CVA is not a support engineer. A CVA is the person who understands what a dealer's platform data is saying and translates that into an account plan alongside the CVM. You will have a defined contribution to the Customer Value Plan and a named role in the Customer Value Review. Your work becomes visible and tied to business outcomes rather than ticket resolution.
Comp How does compensation change? ►
Q1 bonuses are paid at 100% at end of May — the team's strong individual performance was recognized. Q2 transitions to a team-based plan (gross revenue retention and net dollar retention), with potential earnings up to 125% of target. Q3 and Q4 shift to individual-based plans tied to named accounts. Detailed comp plan documentation and FAQs will be distributed to all managers. Review those materials before your team conversations.
If you get questions you can't answer
Escalate to Mark or James directly. Do not speculate on compensation details. The comp documentation is the source of truth.
All roles Who owns my accounts now? Will I have handoffs? ►
The direction is to minimize handoffs over the next 4–6 weeks. Stay the course with current assignments unless there is a critical reason to change. Named account assignments will be formalized by end of April. The goal of the new model is continuity — the opposite of the excessive handoffs that frustrated customers in the current structure.
All roles What is Brett Powell doing here and what will training look like? ►
Brett Powell is an external enablement partner co-facilitating this transformation alongside your leadership team. This is the first formal enablement investment in the team's history. CVM enablement runs the week of May 18th and CVA enablement runs the week of May 25th — Brett co-leads dedicated virtual sessions for each track, paired with a designated leader from the team who brings the real-world context and domain examples that make the frameworks come to life. Sessions are designed to be practical working sessions where the team applies the tools to their own accounts, not presentations.
All roles Is this just a rebrand or is something actually changing? ►
Something is actually changing. The title change is the smallest part of it. What changes is what we are accountable for. A CVM who runs a Customer Value Review instead of a QBR, builds a Customer Value Plan for every account, and tracks dealer maturity over time is doing a fundamentally different job than a CSM who manages escalations and attends renewal calls. The tools being built support that shift. The training in May teaches it. The metrics we agreed to hold us accountable to it.
This is the most important question to answer well
If your team believes this is a cosmetic change, they will not invest in the enablement. Your conviction about the real shift will set the tone.

Section 3 — Enablement Companion

Session-by-session reference for the virtual enablement sessions running weeks of May 18 and 25. Sessions are co-facilitated by Brett and a designated leader for each track — the leader brings field examples and domain knowledge, Brett provides the framework structure. For each session: what the team will cover, the key concepts they need to understand, and your role as their manager in reinforcing what they learn.

1
Dealer Maturity Model
Week of May 18 · CVM and CVA tracks
►
What your team will learn
The five-tier framework for understanding where a dealer is in their Tekion journey — and what CVM and CVA behavior looks like at each tier. Foundation modules (Accounting, Parts, Service) vs. expansion modules (CRM, TekionPay, Analytics, AI) as the key distinction.
  • Four tiers: Operating, Accelerating, Optimizing, Leading
  • The key insight: Tiers are defined by module adoption depth — from under 50% of purchased modules (Tier 1) through full platform adoption and top-quartile performance (Tier 4).
  • Four diagnostic dimensions: Adoption and Usage (CVA-owned) · Value Realization and Self-Sufficiency (CVM-owned)
  • Why it matters: Where a dealer sits in the DMM determines how the CVM and CVA show up. A Tier 1 conversation is not the same as a Tier 4 conversation.
Your role as manager

After the session, ask your team: "Take three of your accounts and tell me which tier you'd place them in today. What's your evidence?" This grounds the model in real accounts rather than theory. If they struggle, it's a coaching signal — not a failure.

2
Customer Value Plan
Week of May 18 · CVM and CVA tracks
►
What your team will learn
The CVP is a living account document — not a one-time intake form. It is the proof that the CVM understands what success looks like for a specific dealer and is tracking it. CVMs author it, CVAs contribute the maturity and adoption sections, and it gets updated when something changes.
  • Phase 1 active sections: Dealer Snapshot (CVM) · Dealer Goals and Success Criteria (CVM) · Maturity and Adoption Status (CVA contributes)
  • Phase 2 sections coming: Value Realized · Value Actions · Risk Flags · Expansion Opportunities
  • When to update: When something changes — not on a fixed calendar. Maturity tier shift, new module live, risk flag, renewal window opens.
  • Why it matters: The CVP without CVA input is a draft. A CVR without a CVP is a generic meeting.
Your role as manager

After the session, inspect one CVP draft from each team member. Not to grade it — to understand where they need help. The most common early gaps: dealer goals that are too vague, maturity tier that hasn't been calibrated with the CVA, and the CVP treated as a form rather than a living document.

3
Touchpoint Framework — Check-In and CVR Cadence
Week of May 25 · CVM track focus
►
What your team will learn
Two touchpoints define CVM engagement: the Customer Value Check-In (relationship call, 20–30 minutes, conversational, monthly or bi-monthly) and the Customer Value Review (structured business meeting, 60–90 minutes, quarterly, with dealer leadership). They are not interchangeable.
  • Check-In purpose: Relationship maintenance, early issue detection, momentum-building. Low prep, low friction, CVM-owned.
  • CVR purpose: Document progress against goals, review adoption, align on next maturity milestone. Significant prep, dealer leadership present.
  • Cadence by tier: Tier 1–2 accounts get monthly Check-Ins. Tier 3–4 accounts get bi-monthly Check-Ins. All tiers get quarterly CVRs except Tier 4 (semi-annual — Leading dealers operate with greater self-sufficiency).
  • The dependency: A CVR cannot be run effectively without an active CVP. The CVP is the pre-meeting brief.
Your role as manager

Review your team's account list by tier. Does the cadence model work given their current account loads? If someone is managing 40+ accounts across all tiers, monthly Check-Ins on Tier 1 and 2 accounts is unrealistic without prioritization rules. That's a coaching and resource conversation, not a compliance conversation.

4
Customer Value Review — Running the Meeting
Week of May 25 · CVM and CVA tracks
►
What your team will learn
The CVR run document takes CVMs from pre-meeting preparation through the five live meeting blocks to the post-meeting CVP update. The CVR is not done until the CVM has updated the CVP. It replaces the QBR with a dealer goal-led conversation, not a Tekion product review.
  • Pre-meeting: CVM reviews dealer goals from CVP. CVA brings adoption snapshot. Intent is set. CVM arrives with a point of view.
  • Five blocks: Open (dealer's world) · Progress (prior commitments) · Value (what the platform is doing for their business) · Next chapter (90-day forward look) · Close (commitments, names, dates)
  • Post-meeting: CVP updated within 24 hours. Seven sections to review. Not complete until the loop closes.
  • The CVR is not a QBR: Dealer goal-led, not Tekion product-led. Business outcomes headline, not usage stats.
Your role as manager

Sit in on one CVR (or a practice run) with each CVM in the first 60 days. Not to evaluate — to understand their comfort level and coaching needs. The most common early challenge: CVMs defaulting to a platform update presentation rather than a dealer goals conversation. That's a facilitation skill, not a knowledge gap.

5
CVA Track — Platform Expertise and CVM Collaboration
Week of May 25 · CVA track
►
What your team will learn
CVA-specific session covering the adoption diagnostic framework, how to prepare the CVA contribution to the CVP, how to present data in a CVR without owning the dealer relationship, and the expansion opportunity identification motion that feeds the Sales handoff.
  • CVA's primary contribution: Section 3 of the CVP — maturity tier, module adoption depth, self-sufficiency assessment, support ticket patterns
  • Data vs. relationship: CVA reads the platform. CVM reads the relationship. Neither can see the full picture alone — that's why the handoff matters.
  • In the CVR: CVA presents data on Blocks 2 and 3. CVM facilitates. Dealer communication routes through CVM outside the CVR.
  • Expansion flag: CVA identifies and documents expansion opportunities. These route to Sales — CVA defines the handoff, not the close.
Your role as manager

After this session, verify that each CVA has a clear line of sight to the CVMs they work with and that the CVP input process is working. The most common early breakdown: CVA does the analysis but CVM doesn't pull it into the CVP before the CVR. That's a process gap, not a skills gap.

Your role as manager — standard week

If those two things are happening, the week is moving in the right direction.

Your role as manager — CVR prep week

The most common early failure mode in CVRs is the CVM walking in without a point of view and the CVA walking in without a current adoption snapshot. What to watch for in the first 60 days

These are the early warning signs that the model is not taking hold. If you see them, it's a coaching conversation — not a performance issue.

  • CVM Check-Ins turning into status calls rather than relationship conversations
  • CVA defaulting to reactive support tickets instead of proactive adoption outreach
  • CVP not being updated after a Check-In or CVR — the loop is not closing
  • CVM and CVA not syncing mid-week — each operating in their own lane
  • CVR agenda defaulting to a Tekion product update rather than a dealer goals conversation
